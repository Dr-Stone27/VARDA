# The Operating Method
**Version 1.0 — Internal & Client-Facing**

---

## What This Document Is

This is the single source of truth for how every engagement works. It exists in two registers simultaneously:

- **For clients:** A clear explanation of how we think, how we work, and why the method produces reliable output
- **For the practice:** The operating standard that every engagement is held to, regardless of who delivers it

Every service we offer — AI Workflow Audit and AI Readiness Assessment — is an application of this same method to a different unit of analysis. The method does not change. The lens does.

---

## The Central Thesis

**AI amplifies whatever you already have.**

If a company has clean data, clear process ownership, and explicit review gates, AI increases throughput and reduces cost. If a company has messy data, unclear accountability, and no fallback logic, AI creates silent failures at scale — wrong outputs reaching customers, errors compounding across weeks before anyone notices.

This thesis is the reason both services exist. The Workflow Audit asks: *where is amplification already producing failures?* The Readiness Assessment asks: *what needs to be true before amplification will work in your favour?*

Different questions. Same underlying logic.

## The Method: Five Stages

Every engagement moves through five stages in sequence. No stage is skipped. Each stage produces a specific output that feeds the next.

---

### Stage 1 — Map

**What happens:** We build a complete, accurate picture of the system before we evaluate it. For a Workflow Audit, the system is the client's current AI tool stack and the workflows those tools touch. For a Readiness Assessment, the system is the client's operational processes — what they are, who owns them, what they cost, and what data feeds them.

**Why it matters:** Most consulting failures happen here — at the mapping stage — because the practitioner accepts the official version of a process rather than the actual one. The official process is what is documented. The actual process is what people do. The gap between them is almost always where the most significant findings live.

**How we do it:** Structured intake form followed by a facilitated 60–90 minute discovery session. We use a fixed inventory template that forces specificity: inputs, outputs, owners, tools, time cost, error rate. We do not move to scoring until the map is complete.

**Output of this stage:** A complete process or touchpoint inventory — every significant item named, described, and attributed.

---

### Stage 2 — Score

**What happens:** Every item in the inventory is scored against a fixed multi-dimension rubric. The rubric encodes the practice's worldview about what makes an AI touchpoint risky or an operational process ready for AI adoption.

**Why it matters:** Scoring converts observation into evidence. Without a rubric, findings are opinions. With a rubric, findings are defensible — every score can be explained, challenged, and updated based on new information. This is what separates a professional assessment from a general consultant's view.

**How we do it:**

- **Workflow Audit:** Five dimensions — data quality, human oversight, fallback logic, output verification, team awareness. Each scored 1–3. Total score classifies the touchpoint as Green (healthy), Amber (at risk), or Red (critical).
- **Readiness Assessment:** Six dimensions — time and cost impact, rule consistency, data readiness, error and rework cost, ownership and change readiness, integration feasibility. Each scored 1–3. Total score classifies the process as Tier 1 (start here), Tier 2 (next wave), or Tier 3 (not yet).

**Output of this stage:** A complete scored inventory with every item classified and the dimension scores documented.

---

### Stage 3 — Decide

**What happens:** We convert the scored inventory into a set of prioritized decisions. Not every finding becomes a recommendation. Not every Tier 1 opportunity becomes an immediate action. The decision stage applies two additional filters: business impact and feasibility.

**Why it matters:** A long list of findings is not a deliverable. A prioritized set of decisions — sequenced, owned, and bounded — is. The client needs to walk away knowing exactly what to do first, what to do next, and what to leave alone. Sequence is itself a form of governance.

**How we do it:** We apply an impact-versus-effort filter to all Red and Amber items (Audit) or all Tier 1 and Tier 2 items (Assessment). We produce a ranked list of no more than eight recommendations. Each recommendation has: a named owner, an estimated timeline, a success metric, and a brief rationale. Items that score well on the rubric but fail the feasibility check are documented as future candidates, not current recommendations.

**The honest call:** If the scoring reveals that a company is not ready to adopt AI anywhere — because data quality or ownership averages are too low across all processes — the decision at this stage is to say so clearly. The recommendation becomes an infrastructure and governance project first. This is not a failure finding. It is the most valuable clarity we can provide.

**Output of this stage:** A prioritized decision list — ranked recommendations with owners, timelines, and metrics.

---

### Stage 4 — Implement

**What happens:** We hand the client a sequenced action plan — 30/60/90 days for Readiness Assessments, 60 days for Workflow Audits — that translates the prioritized decision list into specific actions with specific owners and specific success criteria.

**Why it matters:** A recommendation without an implementation plan is advisory theatre. The client has paid for clarity that leads to change. The implementation plan is the bridge between the finding and the outcome. It also establishes the governance layer — the review cadence, the escalation path, the checkpoints — that makes change sustainable.

**How we do it:** We build the action plan collaboratively in the final session of the engagement. We assign owners by name, not by role. We set success metrics that are measurable within the pilot window. We include a governance checklist — the minimum set of practices that need to be in place before any AI tool goes live or any workflow change is deployed.

**What we do not do:** We do not implement on behalf of the client within the standard engagement scope. Implementation is the client's responsibility. Our role is to make the plan clear enough that implementation does not require us.

**Output of this stage:** A 30/60/90-day or 60-day action plan with named owners, success metrics, and a governance checklist.

---

### Stage 5 — Verify

**What happens:** We define, at the outset of the action plan, how success will be measured. At the end of the pilot window, the client has everything they need to assess whether the changes worked.

**Why it matters:** Without verification, there is no feedback loop — for the client or for the practice. Verification is what turns a one-time engagement into a learning system. It is also what creates the basis for a follow-on engagement: if the Readiness Assessment client implements and wants to audit whether the implementation is working, the Workflow Audit is the natural next step.

**How we do it:** Every recommendation includes a success metric defined at the time of recommendation — not retrospectively. The metric is always binary or directional: did the duplicate ticket rate drop by at least 20%? Is the product description error rate below 5%? Did named ownership get assigned to every AI tool? These are checkable without our involvement.

**Output of this stage:** A verification checklist the client can run themselves at the end of the pilot window — and a brief for any follow-on engagement if one is warranted.

## How the Two Services Differ

The method is identical. The unit of analysis is different.

| | AI Workflow Audit | AI Readiness Assessment |
|--|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Unit of analysis** | Existing AI touchpoints | Operational processes |
| **Core question** | Where is AI already failing silently? | Where should AI be adopted first? |
| **Primary finding type** | Risk and failure classification | Opportunity and readiness classification |
| **Stage 1 output** | AI touchpoint inventory | Process inventory |
| **Stage 2 rubric** | 5-dimension risk rubric | 6-dimension opportunity rubric |
| **Stage 3 output** | Prioritized fix list | Prioritized adoption roadmap |
| **Stage 4 plan** | 60-day pilot plan | 30/60/90-day action plan |
| **Natural follow-on** | Governance review (6 months) | Workflow Audit (12 months post-adoption) |

## The Posture Underneath the Method

The method only works if it is applied with a specific posture. This is not soft — it is operational. The posture has three components:

**Honesty over optimism.** We do not manufacture AI opportunities where none exist. We do not flag every process as automatable. The most valuable finding we sometimes produce is a clear statement of what a company should not pursue, and why. Clients remember this. It is the primary source of referrals.

**Process before technology.** In the vast majority of engagements, the root cause of AI failure or AI unreadiness is not the wrong tool — it is the wrong process, the wrong data, or the wrong ownership. The recommendation almost always begins with a process or governance change, not a tool change. This is what differentiates us from vendors and tool-referral consultants.

**Specificity over completeness.** A short list of specific, owned, measurable actions is worth more than a comprehensive report that nobody acts on. Every deliverable is designed to be used immediately — not filed. Length is not a proxy for value.

## Quality Standard

Every engagement is held to the following minimum standard before the deliverable is produced:

- [ ] Every item in the inventory has a named owner
- [ ] Every score has a documented rationale (not just a number)
- [ ] Every recommendation has a named owner, a timeline, and a success metric
- [ ] At least one item has been explicitly excluded from recommendations with a documented reason
- [ ] The implementation plan is specific enough to be executed without our involvement
- [ ] The governance checklist is included and reviewed with the client before delivery

If any of these are missing, the deliverable is not complete.
