## Part 2 — Five Ways This Quietly Goes Wrong

Patterns we see constantly, across almost every business using AI without a structured setup behind it.

**The Orphaned Tool.** An AI tool gets switched on, works fine for a while, and nobody actually owns it. No one's watching whether it's still doing a good job six months later — until it isn't, and nobody notices for a while.

**The Missing Gate.** AI output goes straight to a customer with no one checking it first. Fine, until the one time it isn't — a wrong claim, an odd tone, a mistake that's already been seen before anyone catches it.

**The Dead End.** The AI hits something it can't handle and just... stops. No handoff, no escalation, no fallback. The customer is left holding the failure.

**The Amplified Mess.** If the information feeding your AI is inconsistent or out of date, the AI doesn't fix that — it repeats it, faster, and at a larger scale than a person ever would.

**The Scaled Inconsistency.** Three people, three different ways of prompting the same task, three different results. No shared standard means no consistent output — no matter how good the tool is.

The Passport you're about to build directly addresses the last two. The first three are process and ownership questions — worth sitting with, even though this kit alone won't solve them.

## Part 3 — A Finished Example

Before you start on your own, here's what a completed Passport actually looks like — start to finish, for a small fictional coffee roaster. Skim it once. It'll make Part 4 faster and a lot less abstract.

```
COMPANY SKILL DOCUMENT
Amble Coffee Co.
Built with the Varda Context Passport | March 2026

---

SECTION 1: WHO WE ARE

Amble Coffee Co. roasts small-batch coffee and ships it before it goes
stale — most bags reach customers within a week of roasting.

We started in 2019 because our founder was tired of grocery-store coffee
that had been sitting on a shelf for months. She began roasting to order
out of a garage and never stopped.

We're proudest of: every bag roasted within 72 hours of shipping, direct
relationships with three family farms, and never blending beans to hide
inconsistent quality.

We never call anything "artisanal" or "gourmet" — our founder can't stand
those words, and neither can our customers.

---

SECTION 2: WHAT WE OFFER

Product: Ethiopia Yirgacheffe
What it is: Light roast, floral and citrusy, best as pour-over
Who it's for: People who already own a pour-over setup and know what they like
Price: $19 / 12oz bag
We can claim: "Notes of jasmine and citrus" (verified by our own cupping notes)
We cannot claim: "Award-winning" — we haven't entered any competitions

Product: House Blend
What it is: Medium roast, our everyday drinker
Who it's for: People who want one reliable bag, not a rotating subscription
Price: $16 / 12oz bag
We can claim: "Our most popular blend, consistent cup after cup"
We cannot claim: "Single origin" — it's a blend of two farms

Common questions:
Q: How fresh is the coffee really?
A: Every bag has a roast date printed on it. We ship within 3 days of roasting, always.

Discontinued: "Decaf Blend" was discontinued in 2024 — do not recommend it.

---

SECTION 3: OUR CUSTOMER

Our customer has upgraded their home brewing setup — pour-over, AeroPress
— and has gotten picky about where the beans come from. They don't want
a subscription from a brand they've never heard of.

They describe the problem as: "I don't want coffee that's been sitting
around."

Common objection: "Is this going to taste burnt or bitter, like most
'craft' coffee?"
Response: "We roast light-to-medium specifically to avoid that — happy
to send a sample bag before you commit to anything bigger."

We're not trying to attract people looking for the cheapest coffee
possible. We're not the budget option, and we don't pretend to be.

---

SECTION 4: HOW WE SOUND

Tone: honest, plain, warm, a little dry.
Never: pretentious, "artisanal," overly poetic, salesy.

We're informal. We use contractions. Occasional dry humour is fine.

Always use: "roasted to order," "roast date."
Never use: "gourmet," "artisanal," "journey" (as in "our coffee journey").

Sounds like us: "This one's for people who think oat milk lattes are a
personality trait. Kidding. Mostly."

Doesn't sound like us: "Indulge in the exquisite journey of our
artisanally roasted, gourmet coffee beans, meticulously sourced to
elevate your daily ritual."

---

SECTION 5: CONSTRAINTS

Never claim "organic" unless the specific bag is certified — only 2 of
our 6 products currently are.

Any complaint about a "bad batch" gets escalated to the founder directly,
not resolved by support alone.

Never reference internal roast batch numbers or supplier pricing.

---

SECTION 6: CURRENT CONTEXT

Tools: Shopify, Klaviyo for email, a shared inbox for support.

Current priorities: launching a subscription option, reducing bag waste
with new packaging, expanding into two new origins.

Right now: Ethiopia Yirgacheffe is sold out until the next shipment in
roughly 3 weeks — do not offer it as in stock.

---

SECTION 7: TASK STANDARDS

Task: Customer support replies
Good: Acknowledges the specific issue, reassures on freshness/roast date, warm not corporate
Bad: Generic "we're sorry for the inconvenience" corporate tone
Special rule: Never promise a refund over $50 without founder approval

Task: Product descriptions
Good: Plain, specific about roast and flavour notes, no overclaiming
Bad: Flowery "journey" language
Special rule: Always include a freshness/roast-date note

Task: Email subject lines
Good: Short, plain, occasionally a little dry or funny
Bad: Excessive emoji, ALL CAPS urgency
Special rule: Never use "LAST CHANCE" or countdown language — doesn't match the brand
```

Notice what makes this work: nothing here is generic. "Never call anything artisanal" is a real, specific rule — not a vague tone description. "Sold out until roughly 3 weeks" is exactly the kind of detail that stops an AI from confidently recommending something that isn't available. That specificity is the whole point. Vague answers produce a vague Passport. Specific answers — even slightly rough ones — produce one that actually works.
