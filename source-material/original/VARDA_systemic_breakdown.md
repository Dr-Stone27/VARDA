# Varda — Systemic Breakdown
**Building Blocks, Standalone Value, and Connecting Threads**
**Working Document — Not for external use**

---

## Why decompose it this way

Varda was built as one continuous system — methodology feeding taxonomy feeding rubrics feeding deliverables feeding pricing feeding pipeline. That continuity is what makes the $5,000 engagement credible. But it also means nobody has ever asked, block by block: *does this piece carry value on its own, separated from everything around it?*

This document answers that question for every component that currently exists. Each block is assessed on four things:

- **What it is** — the artifact itself
- **Standalone value** — would this be useful to someone who never buys the full Varda engagement
- **Dependency** — what it needs to make sense in isolation, if anything
- **Natural packaging** — the format it would take if pulled out and sold or distributed on its own

No recommendations are made here about what to actually sell, bundle, or launch. This is the map. The decision comes after.

| **Standard Taxonomy** (7 failure modes, 7 blockers, 6 touchpoint types, process categories A/B/C) | Named, reusable patterns — "The Orphaned Tool," "The Amplified Mess," etc. | **High.** This is the single most content-rich, most distributable asset in the entire system. | **Scoring Rubrics** (5-dim Audit, 6-dim Assessment) | The scoring mechanics that convert observation into a Green/Amber/Red or Tier 1/2/3 classification | Low-medium alone — a rubric with no judgment applied is just a table. Its value is almost entirely in the *application*, not the artifact. | Needs a practitioner (you) or an engine (the Claude Project) to apply it meaningfully | A self-assessment worksheet (weak), an input to a self-serve tool (stronger) |
| **Governance Primitives** (5 templates: Ownership Register, Review Gate, Fallback Pattern, Measurement Plan, Health Check) | Post-engagement templates a client uses to sustain the work | Medium — genuinely useful, but templates of this type exist broadly in the market; the taxonomy is what makes yours specific, not the primitive itself | Slightly stronger when paired with the taxonomy's named failure modes | A Notion template pack, a recurring "AI governance checklist" lead magnet |

**The one observation worth sitting with:** the taxonomy is doing more standalone work than anything else in the system. It was built as an internal reference tool and it has turned out to be the most externally valuable, most content-native, most easily distributed asset Varda owns.

## Connecting Threads — the dependency map

Some blocks are freestanding. Others only make sense propped up by something else. This matters because it tells you which pieces can leave the nest and which cannot.

**Freestanding — genuinely independent of the rest of Varda:**
- The Standard Taxonomy (each pattern stands alone)
- The values/posture principles
- The intake questionnaires (useful even without the scoring behind them)
- The pricing architecture as a teachable pattern

**Load-bearing — other things depend on these, they depend on little:**
- The Unified Methodology (the taxonomy and rubrics both reference it conceptually)
- The name and thesis (everything inherits tone from these)

**Dependent — need something else to be complete:**
- The scoring rubrics (need a practitioner or engine to apply them)
- Governance primitives (stronger paired with named taxonomy patterns than alone)
- The Claude Project engine (needs the taxonomy and rubrics loaded to say anything useful)
- The sample deliverables (need the rubric and taxonomy to be credible, not just well-written)

**The pattern worth naming:** the further up the stack you go — thesis → taxonomy → rubric → engine → full service — the more *load-bearing but non-sellable* each layer becomes, right up until you hit the full service itself, which is sellable but requires you personally. The taxonomy sits at the sweet spot: independent enough to distribute freely, valuable enough that people would pay for structured access to it, and not so load-bearing that giving it away undermines the paid engagement above it.

That last point matters for the productization question specifically: giving away the taxonomy (as content) does not cannibalize the audit — because the taxonomy tells someone *what the failure patterns are called*, not *which ones they have and what to do about it*. The diagnosis is still the paid part.

## Distribution — the constraint you named

Your personal LinkedIn is committed to the PID — the systems-oriented Enterprise Fintech PM identity, the professional thesis you're building deliberately over time. Varda is a different identity: AI operations advisory for Shopify DTC brands. Running both through one feed blurs a positioning line you've already decided to keep sharp.

That rules out one channel, not the whole plan. Here is the honest map of what's left, without picking one:

**A dedicated Varda presence (LinkedIn Company Page, or a separate personal-adjacent profile/persona).** Keeps the taxonomy content motion alive without touching your PID feed. Costs you: a page has less organic reach than a personal profile algorithmically, and building a second audience from zero is slower than posting to an existing one.

**Communities, not owned feeds** (DTC Slack/Discord groups, the warm-sourcing motion your friend described). This was already the plan for warm client sourcing. It can carry light content too — not broadcast posts, but genuine participation that happens to demonstrate the taxonomy in use.

**X/Twitter.** A different social contract than LinkedIn — more tolerant of a build-in-public, pattern-a-day cadence, and less tied to your existing professional identity since you haven't established a PID-anchored presence there.

**Marketplaces** (Gumroad, a Notion template gallery, PromptBase-style listings). Solves distribution for a *productized* piece (the Skill Doc generator, the self-serve rubric tool) but doesn't solve distribution for the *taxonomy-as-content* motion — marketplaces are where someone lands after they already want the thing, not where discovery happens.

**Newsletter / owned email.** Slower to build, but entirely independent of any platform's algorithm or your personal brand — the cleanest separation from the PID concern, at the cost of needing a subscriber base you don't have yet.

**Referral partners** (Shopify agencies, implementation freelancers like the automation partner). Distribution through other people's existing trust, not your own feed at all — sidesteps the LinkedIn question entirely for the service side, though less obviously useful for distributing a self-serve product.

The tension to hold, not resolve here: the taxonomy content motion works best with a consistent, visible, personality-driven presence — that's what made Temi's post work. A brand-only page or a faceless newsletter has less of that texture. Whether Varda needs *a* face to work as content, and whether that face has to be yours-on-your-main-profile or can be yours-on-a-separate-surface, or someone/something else entirely, is the actual decision sitting inside this constraint.
