# Customer Discovery Plan

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 6 November 2026  
**Status:** Draft for execution after prospect selection

## Objective

Find out whether a specific and repeated AI-enabled workflow problem is sufficiently painful, urgent and economically meaningful for a defined customer to invest in solving it.

This is discovery, not a disguised sales campaign. Do not pitch an unvalidated Varda product.

## Initial research target

As an initial learning target, identify 15–20 carefully selected organisations and seek 5–8 substantive conversations. This is directional discovery, not a statistically representative sample or a market-size estimate.

Test no more than three segment groups initially:

1. DTC/e-commerce businesses (the original Varda beachhead).
2. One adjacent segment with meaningful AI-enabled operational workflows.
3. A second adjacent segment only if it provides a useful contrast.

Select segments by workflow complexity and observable AI activity, not by industry prestige.

## Prospect selection criteria

Prioritise organisations with observable signals such as:

- Publicly described AI or automation deployment in an operational workflow.
- A role responsible for operations, automation, AI, product or systems.
- Multiple software systems or handoffs in the workflow.
- Material customer, transaction or operational volume.
- Public hiring, product releases or process changes that suggest the workflow is important.

Do not infer internal failures from public signals. A signal is a reason to ask a question, not evidence that the prospect has a problem.

## Stakeholders to interview

- Founder / COO: business priority, economics, risk and buying decision.
- Operations leader: actual workflow, exceptions, manual work and ownership.
- Technical / AI / automation owner: system architecture, integration, monitoring, permissions and maintenance.

Do not assume these roles exist in every target company.

## Interview structure (30–45 minutes)

### Opening

Explain that Varda is researching how organisations are using AI in real workflows. Ask permission to take notes. Do not imply the interviewee has a problem.

### Current workflow

1. Walk me through a recent example of this workflow from trigger to final outcome.
2. Which steps involve AI today, and which remain human-led?
3. Which systems, data sources and people are involved?
4. Who owns the workflow and who is responsible when it fails?

### Recent incidents and friction

5. Tell me about the last time this workflow did not behave as expected.
6. What happened next? Who noticed, and how was it resolved?
7. How often does something like that occur?
8. What work is still being done manually around the AI system?
9. What happens if the AI is confidently wrong, unavailable or unable to complete a task?

### Existing alternatives and economics

10. What have you already tried to improve this?
11. Which tools, vendors or internal processes do you rely on?
12. What do those alternatives do well, and where do they fall short?
13. What does the issue cost in time, rework, delay, customer impact or risk? How is that measured?
14. Is there a budget, owner or active initiative attached to this problem?

### Priority and buying behaviour

15. What would need to happen for this to become a priority?
16. Who would need to approve a change?
17. What would a successful improvement look like, and how would you measure it?
18. What would prevent you from adopting an external solution?

### Close

Ask whether the participant would be willing to review a short workflow map or participate in a narrowly scoped follow-up/pilot if a relevant opportunity emerges. Do not imply a pilot is ready before it is defined.

## Interview quality rules

- Ask about the last real occurrence, not hypothetical preferences.
- Do not ask “Would you use/buy this?” as the primary validation method.
- Avoid leading questions about AI risk or agent reliability.
- Capture exact phrases where useful, with permission and without sensitive details.
- Separate facts recalled by the participant from interpretation.
- Record what the participant already does, pays for or has tried.
- Note contradictions and disconfirming evidence.
- Do not treat one articulate participant as representative of a market.

## Outreach workflow

### Step 1 — Prospect intelligence

For each prospect, capture company, segment, role, verified signal, source URL/date, likely workflow relevance, unknowns and a specific reason the person's perspective matters.

### Step 2 — Discovery outreach

Write a brief message based on one verified observation. State that the purpose is to learn how the company handles a relevant workflow. Ask for a short conversation or a specific insight. Do not claim to know an internal problem and do not pitch an unvalidated service.

### Step 3 — Commercial outreach (later)

Only after repeated evidence establishes a problem and Varda has a credible intervention should the original cold-email framework be used. Then personalise around the verified problem, explain the business consequence, show relevant proof, state what Varda will own and use a low-friction CTA.
