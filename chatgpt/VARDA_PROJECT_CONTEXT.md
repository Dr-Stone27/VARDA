# VARDA | Compact Project Context

**Last updated:** 6 November 2026  
**Status:** Initial reset; no product direction selected  
**Name:** Varda is a working name only.

## 1. What the original documents describe

The May 2026 company brief presents Varda as a structured AI operations advisory practice for mid-market e-commerce and DTC businesses. It aims to help companies identify where AI creates value and build the operational foundations to sustain it. The proposed services are:

- **AI Workflow Audit:** examine existing AI touchpoints, score risk across data quality, human oversight, fallback logic, output verification and team awareness, then provide a prioritised fix plan.
- **AI Readiness Assessment:** map significant operational processes, score AI opportunities across time/cost, rule consistency, data readiness, error cost, ownership/change readiness and integration feasibility, then provide a 30/60/90-day roadmap.

Both services use a five-stage method: **Map → Score → Decide → Implement → Verify**. The brief's founding posture is honesty over optimism, process before technology, and actionable recommendations rather than generic maturity scores.

The central thesis in the methodology is: **“AI amplifies whatever you already have.”** Better data, clear ownership and review gates can improve AI outcomes; poor foundations can scale errors and inconsistency.

## 2. Existing components

- Standard taxonomy of AI failure modes, blockers, touchpoint types and process categories.
- AI Workflow Audit and AI Readiness Assessment rubrics.
- Process mapping and discovery workshop framework.
- Governance primitives: ownership, review gates, fallback patterns, measurement plans and health checks.
- Impact estimation framework.
- Context Passport: guided capture of company identity, offer, customers, tone, constraints, current context and task standards, producing a paste-ready business-specific AI context document.
- Productisation hypotheses: self-serve context kit and AI Readiness Snapshot.
- A prospect research and cold-email framework that prioritises prospect understanding, the problem behind the problem, relevant proof, outcome-led positioning and low-friction calls to action.

## 3. Current strategic question

The founder is reconsidering whether the original DTC-centred solution remains relevant as model capability, native platform AI, agents and agent tooling advance. The question is not simply whether AI is growing. It is whether businesses have an urgent, specific problem translating AI capabilities into reliable, integrated and economically valuable workflows, and whether Varda can solve a part of that problem better than existing alternatives.

## 4. Working thesis to test

Businesses may be gaining access to increasingly capable AI systems faster than they are developing the workflows, context, integration, authority boundaries, evaluation and operational ownership required to use those systems reliably and profitably.

This is a hypothesis, not a validated market fact.

## 5. Opportunity hypotheses (none selected)

- **H1 — AI workflow reliability and assurance:** businesses deploying AI workflows/agents struggle to define quality, evaluate behaviour, observe failures, control permissions, manage exceptions and maintain performance.
- **H2 — AI integration and orchestration:** businesses struggle to connect models/agents to existing data, applications, workflows, people and business outcomes.
- **H3 — Business context and knowledge infrastructure:** businesses struggle to supply and maintain authoritative, current, permission-aware context for AI systems across tools and workflows.
- **H0 — Null / incumbent-sufficiency hypothesis:** existing platforms, internal teams or established vendors solve the important parts well enough that a separate Varda offer is not justified.

These hypotheses may overlap, but Varda should not assume one broad platform needs to solve all of them.

## 6. Customer direction

The original beachhead was DTC/e-commerce, particularly Shopify-led businesses. The founder wants to avoid being locked prematurely to one vertical. The current segment hypothesis is therefore based on operational conditions rather than an industry label: businesses with meaningful workflow complexity, real AI usage or near-term deployment intent, connected systems, operational volume, material consequences from errors, and limited internal capacity to make AI dependable.

DTC remains a candidate beachhead because source material already maps its processes. It is not a chosen ICP.

## 7. Important cautions about existing evidence

- The source company brief contains market/adoption statistics and engagement-price claims, but its visible document does not provide sufficient underlying source detail for independent validation. Treat these as **unverified claims**, not facts.
- The market-validation document studies a narrow set of self-serve context/prompt products. It does not validate demand for the core advisory offer or prove willingness to pay.
- The scoring rubrics are useful structured hypotheses, not empirically calibrated measurement instruments.
- Fictional sample deliverables demonstrate how a method could be presented, not actual customer outcomes.
- The documents describe a pre-revenue practice; a first paying customer, repeatability and quantified outcomes have not been established in the supplied material.

## 8. Operating timeline

- **November 2026:** reset first principles; research technology, market and substitutes; conduct discovery; select a customer/problem/workflow; define a credible solution.
- **December 2026:** build or configure a narrow pilot; evaluate it against explicit baselines and criteria; document customer feedback, observed performance, limitations and a build/pivot/stop decision.

## 9. Required discipline

Separate fact, customer-reported information, inference, assumption and unknown. Do not invent evidence. Preserve original files. Do not choose a technology or vertical because it is fashionable. Do not call interviews or prototype completion product-market fit. Progress means reducing uncertainty or demonstrating observed customer value.
