# Varda Opportunity Hypotheses

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 6 November 2026  
**Status:** Research agenda — no winner selected

## Purpose

Compare plausible problems emerging where AI capabilities meet real business workflows. The goal is to identify a specific customer, job and painful unmet need, not to pick a broad market category or design a platform prematurely.

## H1 — AI workflow reliability and assurance

**Hypothesis:** Organisations deploying AI workflows or agents lack effective ways to define expected behaviour, test quality, observe production failures, manage exceptions, constrain actions and maintain performance as models, prompts, data and connected systems change.

**Potential user/buyer:** Operations leader, AI/automation lead, product/engineering lead, risk owner or founder. Must be confirmed by research.

**Possible pain signals:** recurring rework; undetected wrong actions; brittle workflows; limited evaluation; unclear incident ownership; model changes causing regressions; manual review that erases expected efficiency gains.

**Alternatives to investigate:** platform-native monitoring, agent observability/evaluation products, internal engineering, QA processes, manual review, consultants and doing nothing.

**Critical questions:** Is the pain frequent and costly? Who feels it? Who buys? Are current tools insufficient or simply poorly configured? Is a cross-vendor layer needed? What is the narrowest measurable reliability outcome?

**Disconfirming evidence:** existing platforms and internal processes adequately detect and manage failures; incidents are rare or low impact; buyers will not fund a separate solution; reliable measurement is infeasible with available data.

## H2 — AI integration and workflow orchestration

**Hypothesis:** Organisations struggle to connect AI capabilities to fragmented business data, software, people and end-to-end processes, preventing pilots from becoming useful production workflows.

**Potential user/buyer:** Operations leader, transformation lead, systems/integration owner, founder or technical lead.

**Possible pain signals:** manual handoffs between tools; duplicate data entry; agent prototypes that never reach production; integration backlogs; brittle automations; unclear ownership across systems; high implementation and maintenance burden.

**Alternatives to investigate:** native integrations, iPaaS/no-code automation, RPA, systems integrators, vertical SaaS, internal developers and general-purpose agent platforms.

**Critical questions:** Is the problem integration complexity, process ambiguity, data access, permissions or lack of engineering capacity? Is it repeatable across customers? What systems dominate? Is the buyer buying implementation or a reusable product?

**Disconfirming evidence:** native connectors and low-code tools meet needs; each workflow is too bespoke; implementation value does not recur; customer access and security requirements make delivery uneconomic.

## H3 — Business context and knowledge infrastructure

**Hypothesis:** AI systems lack reliable, current, permission-aware business context across tools and workflows, causing inconsistent outputs, incorrect decisions or repeated human effort to supply and maintain information.

**Potential user/buyer:** Knowledge/operations owner, product lead, customer operations lead, AI lead or founder.

**Possible pain signals:** repeated prompting and briefing; duplicated or conflicting knowledge; stale policies/product data; weak retrieval; unclear source of truth; permission leakage; poor context transfer between agents and applications.

**Alternatives to investigate:** native memory/features, knowledge bases, RAG platforms, enterprise search, document management, data platforms, custom context engineering and manual documentation.

**Critical questions:** Is context the root cause or a symptom of poor processes/data? Which information must be current and authoritative? What access controls are needed? How is freshness measured? Who maintains it? What makes a new layer better than existing knowledge tools?

**Disconfirming evidence:** native product features solve the issue; context errors are infrequent or low consequence; the problem is actually missing source data/process ownership; buyers do not perceive a separate budget category.

## H0 — Incumbent-sufficiency / no independent opportunity

**Hypothesis:** Existing software vendors, AI platforms, consultancies and internal technical teams already solve the most important customer needs adequately. Any remaining gap is too fragmented, low urgency or expensive to serve as a standalone business.

This is a serious hypothesis, not a straw man. Research must include reasons Varda should not be built.

## H4 — Services as the initial intervention (business-model hypothesis)

**Hypothesis:** A focused advisory/implementation engagement can help a defined customer solve a painful workflow problem, create trust and generate repeatable learning before software is justified.

This is not itself a customer problem. Test it only after a specific problem and buyer emerge. Compare paid diagnostic, fixed-scope implementation, pilot, managed service and software approaches.

## Comparison framework

Evaluate each hypothesis using the same dimensions. Do not assign scores until scoring anchors and evidence are present.

| Dimension | Evidence to seek |
|---|---|
| Severity | Consequence when the workflow fails or remains unchanged |
| Frequency | Incidents, workarounds or manual effort over a defined period |
| Economic value | Labour, rework, delay, risk, revenue or customer impact; distinguish measured from estimated |
| Urgency | Trigger, deadline, executive priority or existing budget |
| Buyer clarity | Named role, budget control, approval path and procurement constraints |
| Alternative quality | What customers currently use and why it falls short |
| Repeatability | Similar workflow and pain across independent organisations |
| Feasibility | Data access, technical complexity, security and delivery capacity |
| Defensibility | Proprietary workflow insight, integration depth, evaluation data, distribution or switching costs |
| Founder fit | Credible ability to deliver and learn with available resources |

## Research guardrails

- Treat DTC/e-commerce as one comparison segment, not a default winner.
- Limit initial segment exploration to DTC plus at most two adjacent segments.
- Do not infer willingness to pay from survey interest or interview enthusiasm.
- Seek concrete recent examples and existing workarounds.
- Record why each opportunity might not be attractive.
- A score is a synthesis of evidence, not a substitute for it.
