# Stage 1F — Customer/Workflow Discovery Shortlist

**Date:** 7 November 2026  
**Status:** DRAFT — founder review required  
**Purpose:** Recommend a limited set of customer/workflow combinations for primary discovery. This shortlist is for learning only; no customer segment, problem, hypothesis, intervention or commercial model is selected.

## Founder preference and boundary

In the referenced founder-advisor conversation, the founder said Varda began from a consulting perspective and that technical implementation could be embedded in or secondary to that work, rather than making an app the immediate objective. In the current conversation, the founder accepted proceeding on the basis that this is a **strong preference to test**, not a hard customer/problem constraint.

For this shortlist, consulting-led and technology-enabled delivery is therefore a **discovery consideration**, not customer evidence. Investigate whether firms with a concrete workflow issue already seek or use external expertise, what internal and incumbent alternatives they have, and whether the founder's preferred way of working is feasible. Do not pitch a proposed consultancy or ask whether a respondent would buy one.

The four-step “Diagnose / Design / Implement / Assure” structure discussed in that conversation remains an unvalidated service concept. It is not used as a product or service design in this assessment.

## Recommendation

Investigate the following three combinations in parallel as a small, directional first learning cycle. The order reflects learning value and fit with the founder's preferred mode of work, **not** estimated market size, attractiveness or probability of success.

| Priority for learning | Candidate customer/workflow combination | Why it merits investigation | Main counter-case / limitation | Roles to seek |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Professional/business services — a participant-nominated recurring client-delivery or business-administration workflow at a firm that has considered AI but has not adopted it, or is using AI experimentally.** Begin with one named task in its normal path from input to reviewed output; do not presume client-document work is the main pain. | EU professional/scientific/technical firms report higher AI use and consideration than retail proxies (EV-033). UK DSIT reports use in its broader business services/administration category and qualitative accounts of uncertain use cases, limited expertise and informal trials (EV-034). One person may cover multiple roles. |
| 2 | **DTC/ecommerce — order-status, returns or adjacent customer-support work in a retailer that currently uses, is considering, or has stopped/rolled back AI assistance.** Treat “order status/returns” as an inquiry focus from vendor-reported retail use, not a proven DTC pain point. | This tests the historical Varda context without making it the default winner. **Interpretation:** These are concrete interview domains, not three verified opportunity statements. The exact workflow, failure mode and respondent roles must be established by the interviewee. In particular, a well-chosen workflow may produce evidence for H0 (adequate alternatives or no material problem) rather than H1–H3.

## Adoption-status sampling

Recruit across three statuses; do not make public AI activity a prerequisite for inclusion:

1. **Pre-adoption:** considered AI but decided against it, is still exploring, or has no current plan. Include both “considered and declined” and “no identified use/no plan” cases; they are not the same status.
2. **Active adoption:** currently uses AI in the named workflow, including informal trial or pilot. Ask about successful cases and exceptions, not failures only.
3. **Stopped/rolled back:** a pilot was stopped or a live workflow was reduced, rolled back or shut down. Record precisely what stopped, when, why, what continued, and what replaced it. Do not label an organization “AI-abandoned” because it has no current use.

For an initial **5–8 substantive conversations** (the existing plan's directional range), aim to cover all three segment groups and all three statuses if access permits. A practical starting allocation is two conversations per group, with the remaining one or two filling a status gap or probing a contradiction. This is not enough to estimate prevalence or select a segment. If a status cannot be recruited in a group, preserve the gap rather than substitute an unrelated case. Maintain a larger prospect pool than the completed-interview count, but do not contact anyone until the discovery transition is approved.

## Evidence questions for each conversation

### Common core

- Reconstruct the most recent real instance of the workflow from trigger to final outcome, whether AI was involved or not.
- Identify tasks, decisions, people, systems, data/context, permissions, handoffs, review points and exceptions.
- Ask what worked as expected as well as what required correction, delay, manual work or escalation.
- For a concrete issue, determine frequency and consequence using observed or recorded measures where available; separate measured impact from estimates.
- Record alternatives actually used (including manual process, embedded tools, internal staff, consultants, vendors, or doing nothing), what each handles adequately, and why a change was or was not made.
- Identify the workflow owner, decision-maker, budget authority, recent trigger and prior spending/effort, without asking hypothetical willingness-to-pay questions.
- Ask whether external expertise was previously sought or bought and what it did. Treat founder preference as separate from customer behaviour.

### Status-specific probes

| Status | Ask about | Avoid assuming |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-adoption | The specific task considered; the decision and evidence behind it; who explored tools; what was tried instead; why the business proceeded, declined or deferred; what would change the decision. | That no adoption means an unmet need, lack of awareness, or willingness to pay for guidance. |
| Active | What AI does in one workflow today; which outputs/actions are checked; a recent success and a recent exception; how systems, source material, permissions and handoffs work; maintenance time and actual outcomes. | That all AI use is production use, autonomous, integrated, valuable or unreliable. |
| Stopped/rolled back | What exactly was stopped and at what deployment stage; the initiating event; who detected/decided; the sequence of root-cause investigation; immediate consequence; remaining use and replacement process. | That stopping one workflow means abandoning AI overall, or that the stated cause proves a single primary constraint. |

## Hypothesis discrimination and counter-evidence

Use the incident-level distinctions in [`hypothesis_assessment_2026-11-07.md`](../01_market/hypothesis_assessment_2026-11-07.md). For every candidate, keep these alternatives open:

- **H1 — Reliability/assurance:** output/action quality or exception detection failed despite needed inputs and integrations being available.
- **H2 — Integration/orchestration:** a named handoff, system access, permission, state or maintenance boundary blocked the workflow.
- **H3 — Context/knowledge:** authoritative information was missing, stale, conflicting, inaccessible or not retrieved, and correcting it would change the result.
- **H0 — Incumbent sufficiency/no venture:** existing software, internal teams/providers, manual review, or no action handles the job within acceptable quality, time, risk and cost.
- **Pre-adoption opportunity:** the business has an actual workflow and a decision it has tried to make, but has not identified a worthwhile AI-enabled change. If no recurring pain or useful change exists, record that as counter-evidence.

Do not infer that the preferred delivery model is needed merely because a problem exists. A customer may solve it with internal staff, native tools, a vendor, an existing consultant, a process change, or no change.

## Discovery-plan readiness and required guardrails

The current [`customer_discovery_plan.md`](customer_discovery_plan.md) has a sound non-sales interview structure and directional sample size, but its original prospect criteria prioritize publicly visible AI deployment and “observable AI activity.” Used without balancing, those criteria would over-sample active adopters and miss businesses that rejected AI or see no reason to adopt. The plan has been adjusted to include status-based recruitment and neutral, status-specific interview probes. It remains a draft; this recommendation does **not** authorize prospect outreach or interviews.

Before the founder approves customer discovery, confirm:

1. The intended geography and reachable founder network; current public evidence is mainly UK/EU and does not identify where Varda can sell.
2. Whether the initial 5–8 interviews are a first learning cycle only; they cannot support segment prevalence or a winner.
3. Whether prospects can be recruited in more than one adoption status without relying on public claims about internal operations.
4. A privacy-safe consent and note-storage approach, including what customer materials will not be collected.

## Completion and gate recommendation

**Finding:** The current evidence supports a limited set of discovery probes, not an opportunity ranking. Professional/business services pre-adoption is the clearest way to test the founder's preference for consulting-led opportunity identification; DTC customer operations retains value as a comparison to the original thesis; software/technology production workflows provide a strong H0 challenge around internal technical capacity and existing alternatives.

**Confidence:** Low for claims about these exact workflow/customer combinations because no direct customer evidence exists. Medium that the combinations are reasonable learning candidates given the bounded sector/adoption evidence recorded in EV-033–EV-035 and the alternatives map.

**Counter-evidence:** Businesses may report no material workflow problem; existing platforms, internal teams or service providers may be sufficient; high AI usage may indicate capability, not pain; some non-adoption may be rational; small firms may not resemble the enterprise evidence.

**Stage 1F conditions:** Three approved segment groups are represented; status diversity and non-adopter cases are explicit; each combination names a bounded inquiry focus, reasons to investigate, counter-cases and relevant roles; the consulting preference is separated from customer demand; no segment or hypothesis is chosen.

**Status:** Conditions are met for founder review. No outreach, interviews, prospect contact, service design or implementation has begun.

**Recommended next stage:** Customer discovery, after separate founder approval. If approved, begin with a first learning cycle of 5–8 conversations using the balanced status sample above, then report the cases, contradictions and coverage gaps before deciding whether to broaden, narrow or stop.
