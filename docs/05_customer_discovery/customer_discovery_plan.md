# Customer Discovery Plan

**Version:** 0.2
**Date:** 7 November 2026
**Status:** Draft for execution after founder approves transition to customer discovery

## Objective

Find out whether a specific business workflow presents a repeated or consequential problem, whether AI has a real role in the workflow or was considered and declined, and whether the business is investing effort or money to address the issue. Do not presume that a workflow needs AI or that a problem requires external help.

This is discovery, not a disguised sales campaign. Do not pitch an unvalidated Varda product.

## Initial research target

As an initial learning target, identify 15–20 carefully selected organisations and seek 5–8 substantive conversations. This is directional discovery, not a statistically representative sample or a market-size estimate.

Compare only the three segment groups already approved for opportunity discovery:

1. DTC/e-commerce businesses (historical Varda beachhead; not selected).
2. Business/professional services.
3. Software/technology.

Do not treat the sector groups as exact official categories or assume a segment is attractive based on adoption rates. Select cases for information value and reachable access; do not select only for workflow complexity or visible AI activity.

## Prospect selection criteria

Seek variation in adoption status across the three groups. Include, where accessible:

- Businesses that considered AI for a real workflow but declined or deferred it.
- Businesses that report no identified use or no current plan, to test H0 and avoid assuming demand.
- Businesses currently using AI in a named workflow, including informal trials and pilots.
- Businesses that stopped a pilot or reduced/rolled back a live AI workflow.

Use verified public signals where available, such as:

- Publicly described AI or automation deployment in an operational workflow.
- A role responsible for operations, automation, AI, product or systems.
- Multiple software systems or handoffs in the workflow.
- Material customer, transaction or operational volume.
- Public hiring, product releases or process changes that suggest the workflow is important.

Do not require a public AI signal to include a prospect. Do not infer internal use, failure or adoption status from public signals; verify status with the participant. A signal is a reason to ask a question, not evidence that the prospect has a problem.

## Stakeholders to interview

- Founder / COO: business priority, economics, risk and buying decision.
- Operations leader: actual workflow, exceptions, manual work and ownership.
- Technical / AI / automation owner: system architecture, integration, monitoring, permissions and maintenance.
- In professional services, a managing partner, service-line owner or operations lead may be relevant; in ecommerce, an owner/COO, customer-support/CX lead or platform owner; in software/technology, a product/engineering or AI lead and the operational owner. These are role hypotheses, not assumptions that every firm has these titles.

Do not assume these roles exist in every target company.

## Interview structure (30–45 minutes)

### Opening

Explain that Varda is researching how organisations handle important business workflows and how they decide whether to change them. AI may or may not be involved. Ask permission to take notes. Do not imply the interviewee has a problem.

### Current workflow

1. Walk me through the most recent example of this workflow from trigger to final outcome, whether or not AI was involved.
2. What role, if any, did AI play? If none, has it been considered for this task? What did the business decide?
3. Which systems, data sources and people are involved?
4. Who owns the workflow and how are exceptions or changes handled?

### Recent incidents and friction

5. Tell me about a recent time this work followed expectations, or needed correction, rework, delay or escalation. What happened?
6. Who noticed? What happened next, and how was it resolved or completed?
7. How often does something like that occur?
8. Which steps are manual, automated or AI-assisted? Where do people add work, if anywhere?
9. If AI is used or was tried, what happens when it is wrong, unavailable or cannot complete a task? If it is not used, what happens when the current process hits an exception?

### Existing alternatives and economics

10. What have you already tried to improve this?
11. Which tools, vendors or internal processes do you rely on?
12. What do those alternatives do well, and where do they fall short?
13. What does the issue cost in time, rework, delay, customer impact or risk? How is that measured?
14. Is there a budget, owner or active initiative attached to this problem?

### Priority and buying behaviour

15. What would need to happen for this to become a priority?
16. Who would need to approve a change?
17. What would a successful improvement look like, and how would you measure it?
18. What would prevent you from changing the current process or involving an external provider? Have you actually used or paid an external provider for this or related work before? What happened?

### Adoption-status probes

Use only the probes relevant to the participant; do not assume these statuses indicate a problem.

- **Considered / deferred / declined:** What exact task was considered, what alternatives were tested, who made the decision, and what evidence or trigger would change it?
- **No current use or plan:** How is the workflow handled now? What makes the status quo adequate or not worth changing? What would be a credible reason to reconsider?
- **Active use / pilot:** What is the AI doing in the named task? Ask for a recent success and exception, review process, actual maintenance effort and measured outcome.
- **Stopped / rolled back:** What exactly stopped and when? Was it a pilot or live workflow? What event and decision led to stopping, what use continued, what replaced it, and what was the consequence?

### Close

Ask whether the participant would be willing to review a short workflow map or participate in a narrowly scoped follow-up/pilot if a relevant opportunity emerges. Do not imply a pilot is ready before it is defined.

## Interview quality rules

- Ask about the last real occurrence, not hypothetical preferences.
- Do not ask “Would you use/buy this?” as the primary validation method.
- Avoid leading questions about AI risk or agent reliability.
- Capture exact phrases where useful, with permission and without sensitive details.
- Separate facts recalled by the participant from interpretation.
- Record what the participant already does, pays for or has tried.
- Distinguish no adoption, considered-and-declined, active use, pilot stopped and production rollback. Never treat “no use/no plans” as abandoned adoption.
- Recruit across pre-adoption, active-adoption and stopped-use cases; if a status cannot be recruited within a segment, record the gap rather than infer it.
- Note contradictions and disconfirming evidence.
- Do not treat one articulate participant as representative of a market.

## Outreach workflow

### Step 1 — Prospect intelligence

For each prospect, capture company, segment, role, verified signal, source URL/date, likely workflow relevance, unknowns and a specific reason the person's perspective matters.

### Step 2 — Discovery outreach

Write a brief message based on one verified observation. State that the purpose is to learn how the company handles a relevant workflow. Ask for a short conversation or a specific insight. Do not claim to know an internal problem and do not pitch an unvalidated service.

### Step 3 — Commercial outreach (later)

Only after repeated evidence establishes a problem and Varda has a credible intervention should the original cold-email framework be used. Then personalise around the verified problem, explain the business consequence, show relevant proof, state what Varda will own and use a low-friction CTA.
