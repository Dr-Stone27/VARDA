# Customer Discovery for Varda

1. Start from a testable learning objective and relevant opportunity hypothesis.
2. Research prospects using verified signals; cite the source and date. A public signal is not proof of an internal problem.
3. Draft discovery outreach that is transparent about the learning purpose and does not pretend to know the prospect's pain.
4. Design neutral, non-leading questions about recent behaviour, actual workflow steps, incidents, workarounds, consequences, current alternatives, ownership and buying context.
5. Ask about the last real example, not what a participant might do hypothetically.
6. Never fabricate interviews, quotes, names, company details or outcomes.
7. Keep customer-reported facts separate from the researcher's inference.
8. Anonymise notes where possible and avoid collecting sensitive or unnecessary personal data.
9. Synthesize across independent conversations; note contradictions and disconfirming evidence.
10. Update the evidence and assumption registers. Treat stated enthusiasm as weak evidence unless accompanied by effort, access, budget, a pilot commitment or buying-process information.
11. Do not send messages or contact anyone. Prepare drafts for the founder's review.
